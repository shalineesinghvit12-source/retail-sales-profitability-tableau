# Retail Sales & Profitability Dashboard

An interactive Tableau portfolio project focused on retail sales performance, revenue, production cost relationships, customer/product segmentation, and geographic sales distribution.

## Live Tableau Dashboard

[View the interactive dashboard on Tableau Public](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/shalinee.singh6168/viz/Shalinee-SinghAssignment/Dashboard3)

## Project Objective

The goal of this project is to turn retail operational data into clear visual insights that support product, sales, and business-performance decisions.

The dashboard is designed to answer questions such as:

- Which product types sell the highest number of units?
- How does product demand vary by gender?
- What relationship exists between production cost and production volume?
- Which locations generate the strongest product demand?
- How do revenue and number of products sold compare across product types and customer segments?

## Dashboard Components

### Product Sales by Product Type and Gender
A stacked bar chart compares the number of products sold across product types and gender segments, making differences in product demand easy to identify.

### Cost vs Production Volume
A scatter plot explores the relationship between production cost and production volume and helps surface patterns, clusters, and potential outliers.

### Geographic Sales Distribution
A map shows the number of products sold by location, helping identify geographic areas with stronger or weaker sales activity.

### Revenue and Sales Volume by Product Type
A comparative bar chart evaluates revenue and number of products sold for each product type by gender, connecting sales volume with business value.

## Business Value

The dashboard brings product, customer-segment, operational-cost, geographic, and revenue information into a single analytical view. It can support decisions around product focus, market targeting, production planning, and sales strategy.

## Interview Explanation

I built this Tableau dashboard to analyze retail performance from several perspectives instead of relying on a single sales metric. I compared product demand by product type and gender, explored the relationship between production cost and volume, mapped sales geographically, and compared revenue with units sold. The goal was to create a dashboard where a reviewer can move from high-level sales patterns to operational and segment-level insights and make evidence-based business decisions.
